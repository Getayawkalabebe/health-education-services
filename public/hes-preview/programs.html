Patient support

Diabetes management

Make diabetes care easier to navigate between appointments. Start with the part of your routine that feels hardest, from understanding a glucose report to preparing for a change in schedule.

Who it’s for

Adults living with diabetes who want practical education after diagnosis or when their routines or care plans change.

What you’ll cover

  • Review the questions you have about your care plan
  • Practice spotting patterns in readings and writing down useful context
  • Build a routine for meals, activity or monitoring that fits your day
  • Prepare questions about targets, symptoms or medicines for your care team

How it can work

In an individual education conversation, bring a typical day and one challenge you want to work on. Together, you can map the challenge, choose a manageable action and discuss what you would review at a follow-up.

What you can take away

A personal learning goal, an everyday action to try and a short list of questions for your next care visit. Your clinical team sets treatment and medication plans.

Patient support

CGM coaching

Turn a continuous glucose monitor (CGM) report into a conversation you can use. Learn to read trends in context and decide which questions are worth taking to your care team.

Who it’s for

Adults using a CGM or considering one, and support people invited by the person using it.

What you’ll cover

  • Get familiar with trend arrows, alerts and time in range
  • Compare patterns across several days alongside meals, movement or schedule changes
  • Practice summarizing one recurring pattern and sharing a report if you choose
  • Identify device questions to check against manufacturer instructions or discuss with your care team

How it can work

Walk through a sample report or review a report you choose to share during a session. Start with one question, such as why a pattern seems to repeat after a particular meal, and note what else was happening at the time.

What you can take away

A plain-language summary of one pattern and questions to discuss at your next visit. Ask separately about device availability, costs and compatibility.

Patient support

Sustainable weight management

Build eating and movement habits you can keep through busy weeks, travel and changes in routine. Start with goals that matter to you, such as planning meals more easily or making time for movement.

Who it’s for

Adults seeking weight-related education and habit support that fits their health needs, preferences and budget.

What you’ll cover

  • Choose a goal you can act on and track
  • Plan grocery and meal options around your schedule and food preferences
  • Choose movement options suited to your ability and circumstances
  • Practice adjusting your plan after a difficult week

How it can work

An individual coaching discussion can begin with a typical week. Identify a barrier, try one small change and use a follow-up conversation to decide what to keep or adjust.

What you can take away

A weekly action plan, a way to track the habit and a backup option for difficult days. Discuss individualized medical nutrition therapy or treatment options with a qualified clinician.

Caregiver and family support

Support someone living with diabetes while respecting their choices. Learn how to ask what help they want, talk about routines without blame and share responsibilities in ways that work for everyone.

Who it’s for

Family members, friends and caregivers, with the person living with diabetes invited to guide the conversation when they wish.

What you’ll cover

  • Ask what support is welcome and when
  • Agree on practical roles for shopping, appointments or reminders
  • Discuss privacy and consent before sharing glucose information
  • Prepare questions about the care team’s action plan and whom to call

How it can work

A family education session can use a common scenario, such as sharing meals or preparing for a trip. Practice a conversation and agree on what each person will handle.

What you can take away

A shared support plan with boundaries, agreed roles and care-team questions.

Community support

Weight & Nutrition Workshops

Give participants time to practice food skills they can use at their next grocery trip or meal. Workshops can be tailored to the foods people eat, the stores they use and the time they have to cook.

Who it’s for

Community centers, faith groups, employers and organizations hosting adult learning sessions.

What you’ll cover

  • Compare food labels using realistic shopping examples
  • Plan a meal or grocery list within a budget
  • Explore flexible portions and substitutions for familiar meals
  • Choose one practical change and a way to check how it went

How it can work

Plan a demonstration, a hands-on small-group activity and a short take-home exercise. Hosts can discuss venue, language, accessibility, audience size and materials with HES.

What you can take away

Participants can leave with a sample shopping plan and one action to try. Hosts can use a short activity or feedback form to see which skills need more practice.

Community support

Prediabetes Screening

Help people understand their risk for prediabetes and take a clear next step toward appropriate testing and follow-up.

Who it’s for

Community organizations and clinical partners planning outreach for adults who may benefit from discussing screening.

What you’ll cover

  • Explain what a risk questionnaire can tell someone
  • Prepare participants to ask a clinician about appropriate testing
  • Provide private, plain-language instructions for follow-up
  • Identify referral options and barriers such as transportation, cost and language

How it can work

Plan the event around education, private conversations and a referral pathway. If testing is proposed, confirm the clinical partner, consent process, testing responsibilities, result communication and follow-up before advertising it.

What you can take away

A clear next-step handout and referral plan. A risk questionnaire is an education tool. Qualified clinicians handle testing, diagnosis and follow-up.

Community support

Active Living Initiatives

Make movement easier to start and repeat in the places people already gather. Build activities around what participants enjoy and what their community can support.

Who it’s for

Neighborhood groups, community centers and organizations serving adults with a range of abilities and activity levels.

What you’ll cover

  • Identify practical barriers such as cost, safety or transportation
  • Explore walking, seated or indoor options as appropriate
  • Choose an achievable activity goal and a backup plan
  • Build a peer check-in or recurring group activity

How it can work

Hosts and HES can design an introductory activity, map accessible options and agree on a follow-up. Plan for seating, weather, rest breaks and clear guidance on who should consult their care team before participating.

What you can take away

A realistic movement plan for participants and a simple delivery checklist for hosts. Participation and feedback can guide improvements.

Community support

Youth & Family Health Literacy Workshop

Help young people and their families understand everyday health messages, ask questions and practice making informed choices together.

Who it’s for

Schools, youth organizations and family groups seeking age-appropriate activities with caregiver involvement.

What you’ll cover

  • Practice reading a food label or comparing drink options
  • Tell the difference between a health claim and its source
  • Practice asking a trusted adult or clinician a health question
  • Choose a family activity or meal-planning habit to try together

How it can work

Use short scenarios, demonstrations and a family take-home activity. Agree with hosts on age range, accessible materials and caregiver consent or participation requirements.

What you can take away

A practical family activity and a simple way for participants to explain what they learned. Activities focus on skills and avoid public weigh-ins or body comparisons.

Consulting

Corporate Wellness Advisory

Design a workplace health education effort that employees can access across shifts, locations and job roles.

Who it’s for

Employers, human resources teams and benefits leaders defining or improving a wellness initiative.

What you’ll cover

  • Assess priorities and barriers with employee input
  • Set learning objectives and select practical topics
  • Plan delivery across shifts and locations
  • Choose participation and feedback measures that protect individual privacy

How it can work

Begin with a discovery meeting and a review of the current offer. Agree on the audience, scope, communication approach and responsibilities before proposing a rollout.

What you can take away

A proposed implementation brief with audience priorities, delivery options, a rollout sequence and measures for review.

Consulting

Provider Continuing Medical Education Workshops

Give healthcare teams a structured opportunity to practice clearer conversations about diabetes, glucose reports and self-management education.

Who it’s for

Clinics, health systems and professional organizations planning education for clinicians or interdisciplinary teams.

What you’ll cover

  • Identify a practice gap and write measurable learning objectives
  • Use cases to practice discussing glucose trends and care-team questions
  • Use teach-back to check how clearly an explanation landed
  • Identify a referral or communication improvement to try after the workshop

How it can work

Agree on audience, clinical content, faculty responsibilities and evaluation with the host. A case-based session can combine a short presentation, group discussion and a practical exercise.

What you can take away

Learning objectives, a draft agenda and an evaluation plan. If CME credit is requested, an accredited provider must review and approve the activity before credit is advertised.

Consulting

Public Health Strategy

Turn a community health priority into a program that partners can deliver, measure and improve.

Who it’s for

Public agencies, nonprofits, coalitions and community organizations planning or refining an education or prevention initiative.

What you’ll cover

  • Define the audience and the problem with community input
  • Map assets, service gaps and referral pathways
  • Connect activities to intended short- and longer-term outcomes
  • Set implementation roles, milestones and measures for review

How it can work

Scope the work together. A project may include a document review, listening sessions, a partner planning meeting and a review of the draft design. Confirm data access and responsibilities early.

What you can take away

Depending on the agreed scope, a program design brief, logic model, implementation roadmap and evaluation measures that partners can use to guide decisions.
